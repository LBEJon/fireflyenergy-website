MOCKUP — Hybrid Inverter vs. Microinverter (Light)
Hybrid Inverter VS Microinverter

Same sun. Same energy.
One superior solution.

Solar panels with microinverters produce energy and lower your energy costs — but that's where the benefits stop. Firefly's hybrid architecture creates a microgrid in your home, delivering true energy independence even when CFE goes down.

01

What happens when CFE goes out

The moment the grid drops, the system decides whether your home stays powered.

● Firefly Hybrid
Keeps producing and delivering power from solar and battery. Your home stays on.
○ Microinverter
Shuts off. Grid-tie code requires microinverters to stop producing when CFE goes down — even with full sun on the panels.
Why it matters This is the single biggest functional difference. A microinverter system alone cannot power your home during a blackout.
02

Backup battery integration

Backup as a built-in feature vs. something bolted on later.

● Firefly Hybrid
Built in from day one. Out-of-the-box compatible with DC batteries, and you can connect multiple packs in parallel to scale capacity as your needs grow.
○ Microinverter
Not native to the solution. Adding backup requires a separate battery system and a coordinating gateway — often from different brands stitched together.
Why it matters One integrated system means simpler wiring, one 8-year warranty, and one local team accountable for the whole installation.
03

Where the electronics live

Location shapes lifespan and servicing effort.

● Firefly Hybrid
One wall-mounted inverter inside your home or garage — ventilated, shaded, and serviced at eye level.
○ Microinverter
3–5 microinverters on the roof for a typical 6–10 panel install (one per pair of panels), exposed to sun and weather.
Why it matters Rooftop electronics weather faster than equipment indoors, and service calls require roof access and panel removal.
04

Expanding the system later

Both systems can grow — they just scale different things.

● Firefly Hybrid
Designed to grow with you. Add battery capacity up to 16 units in parallel, add panels when you need to generate more energy, or add additional inverters to scale as your needs grow.
○ Microinverter
Solar expands, storage doesn't. Each new panel needs a compatible microinverter; mixed panel models are fine if each is paired correctly. Adding backup still requires a separate battery system later.
Why it matters Firefly's upgrade path includes more storage — the part most homeowners actually want to add as CFE reliability changes.
